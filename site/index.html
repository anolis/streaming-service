KNOW BEFORE YOU CAST

Different TVs.
One place to start.

This is a young project, tested on real devices. Protocol support varies—here’s what works and what’s still taking shape.

Available01

Chromecast

Screen mirroring, local files, and media URLs. Automatically transcodes local formats the receiver can’t decode.

HLS mirroring · about 10 s delay by default
Available02

DLNA

Screen mirroring and media playback for compatible smart TVs. Tested with a Samsung TV; files are served in their original format.

MPEG-TS mirroring · receiver controls buffering
In development03

AirPlay

A native mirroring prototype has displayed a test pattern on a Samsung TV. Desktop capture, audio, and broader compatibility remain under validation.

Development preview · not in the published installer
External app04

Miracast

Launch GNOME Network Displays from the menu. Choose and control your receiver there. Native Miracast support is not implemented yet.

Requires GNOME Network Displays + Wi-Fi Direct

MAKE IT FIT

Sharper picture.

Mirroring settings are saved in the desktop interface and apply to the next cast. Start with the defaults, then adjust to suit your connection.

Lower buffers can stutter on unreliable Wi-Fi. The buffer setting applies to HLS playback; DLNA buffering is controlled by the TV.

A FEW PRACTICAL DETAILS

Before you press play.

Which Linux desktops are supported?

Cinnamon has a native panel applet. Xfce has a tray icon and flyout. The command line also works independently. The current screen capture path requires an X11 session.

Is this suitable for gaming?

Chromecast mirroring uses HLS and deliberately keeps a playback cushion. Its default delay is around 10 seconds, so it suits watching and presenting better than interactive gaming. The native AirPlay work aims to provide another transport, but measured latency is not yet available.

Why isn’t my TV appearing?

Make sure the computer and receiver are on the same reachable local network, enable casting on the TV, and refresh the device list. Network isolation can block discovery. HTTP-based casting also needs the TV to reach a randomly selected port on your computer.

Does every AirPlay TV support video URLs?

No. Pairing and URL-video support are separate capabilities. The tested Samsung rejects URL playback, which is why native screen mirroring is being developed as a separate backend.

Where can I report a problem?

Use the repository’s issue tracker. Include your Linux distribution, desktop, receiver model, protocol, and the error message. Avoid posting pairing credentials or private media.
